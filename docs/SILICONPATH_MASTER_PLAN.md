## Executive brief
SiliconPath is an independent semiconductor education, planning and volunteer project-support initiative. This plan preserves its next development direction within the existing SiliconPath project: an online collaboration workspace, a small coordinating office and two complementary future labs.

Mission: connect semiconductor learning with documented design, supervised prototyping and reproducible research.
Vision: a sustainable pathway from online study to practical engineering capability and partner-supported projects.

The existing educational platform is the starting asset. Dedicated premises, staff, equipment, funding, service revenue and institutional partnerships are not established by this plan. No institutional endorsement is implied. Timing is relative to an agreed kickoff, available capacity and confirmed resources.

## Proposed two-lab model
### Lab 1 — Digital Design, Simulation and AI
Start online with the contributors' existing computers and tools whose availability and terms have been checked.
Activities: requirements, circuit/PCB design studies, simulation, embedded software, synthetic-data AI demonstrations, reproducibility and packaging education models.
Outputs: design files, documented assumptions, review notes, experiment configurations and demonstration reports.
Boundary: simulations are labeled as simulations; software results do not establish physical performance.

### Lab 2 — PCB Assembly, Test and Packaging Development
Begin with planning and access discussions. Introduce physical work only after the host confirms appropriate space, equipment, supervision and operating requirements.
Initial candidate activities: supervised board assembly, inspection, electrical testing and prototype documentation.
Packaging track: package comparison, chip-to-board interconnection studies, thermal concepts and partner-led demonstrations.
Advanced packaging fabrication is a later capability requiring specialist review and a separate facility and equipment assessment. PCB assembly and semiconductor packaging must remain distinct in training and capability claims.
Outputs: approved test procedures, assembly records, measured reports and capability assessments.

### Shared project office
A coordination function can start remotely. A dedicated office is a later resource decision.
Maintain a project inbox, weekly agenda, decision log, milestone tracker, resource register and controlled document index. Use approved meeting space when available. Personal building access does not establish authorization for new equipment or lab operations.

## Flagship pilot — Sensor-to-System
Purpose: connect a small sensor-board design study with an explainable, reproducible AI demonstration.
Phase A is digital and does not require purchasing hardware.
1. Agree one sensing use case, intended audience and measurable requirements.
2. Produce a block diagram, preliminary schematic and design assumptions.
3. Implement a synthetic-data baseline and small ML comparison with fixed seeds and separated evaluation data.
4. Record metrics, limitations and reproduction instructions; do not describe synthetic outcomes as sensor validation.
5. Prepare the PCB review package, packaging explainer and proposed physical test plan.
6. Obtain technical feedback and write a next-phase decision.

Digital acceptance: a fresh checkout reproduces the documented demo; sources and assumptions are traceable; design review issues are recorded; the report distinguishes digital results from unperformed physical tests.
Physical extension: confirmed access, reviewed design, agreed resource budget and approved operating arrangements before assembly or testing. Report actual measurements with conditions and limitations.

## Resources and funding
Start with an inventory, not a shopping list. Record each resource as confirmed available, access requested, quote required or future.
Maintain separate requests for supervised access, in-kind support and cash funding. Every request identifies a milestone, owner, expected output and acceptance criterion.
Budget categories: tools and software; design review; fabrication and components; assembly and inspection; test access; space and utilities; training; consumables; maintenance; shipping and applicable charges.
No amounts are asserted here. Record dated quotations, quantities, recurring costs, exclusions and funding source before approving a purchase. Include a visible contingency assumption when preparing a budget.
Possible support routes to investigate include university-approved pilot opportunities, partner access, donations and sponsored projects. Eligibility, conditions and actual availability remain unverified.
Paid services are a future decision requiring demonstrated capability and agreed legal, institutional and financial arrangements; no revenue forecast is claimed.

## Operating rhythm
- Weekly: review completed evidence, blockers, next tasks and contributor capacity.
- Per task: record owner, output, acceptance criterion, dependency and due date.
- Per milestone: review scope, evidence, resource use and decision to continue.
- Per release: preserve source version, report, limitations and handover instructions.

Track completed and accepted outputs, reproducibility, unresolved review issues, confirmed resource access and actual spend. Do not equate website visits or team size with engineering readiness.
