# Semiconductor Starter Path

Status: initial draft; subject review pending. Prepared 2026-10-14.

## Outcome and prerequisites

Using a browser and a notebook, explain the journey from an electronic design to a chip used on a board. Produce a one-page process map and a next-learning plan. No hardware purchase or laboratory access is needed.

Suggested sequence and effort: four sessions of 45–60 minutes.

## 1. Learn the vocabulary

| Term | Introductory meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Semiconductor | A material whose electrical behavior can be controlled for electronic applications |
| Wafer | A substrate on which many chip structures can be manufactured |
| Die | An individual chip cut from a processed wafer |
| Package | A structure that protects a die and provides connections to the outside system |
| PCB | A printed circuit board that connects components within an electronic assembly |
| Metrology | Measurement used to characterize materials, structures, and processes |

Read ASML's introductory chipmaking overview [S1]. Write your own definitions, then distinguish die, package, and PCB using a labeled sketch.

## 2. Map the journey

| Stage | Example input | Example output | Question to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design | Functional requirements | Circuit/layout information | How is intended behavior verified? |
| Wafer fabrication | Wafer and process information | Patterned device structures | What role does lithography play? |
| Packaging | Individual die and package materials | Connected, protected device | How does the package connect to a board? |
| PCB assembly | Board and components | Populated electronic assembly | How does board assembly differ from making a die? |
| Testing and measurement | A device or assembly and a test plan | Measurements and acceptance evidence | What property is being checked? |

This is a simplified teaching map, not a production flow. Testing and measurement occur at multiple stages. Actual manufacturing includes repeated operations, feedback, and product-specific steps.

ASML explains that chip structures are built through patterned layers on a wafer and describes lithography, deposition, etching, and other stages [S1]. Explain those terms at a conceptual level; do not infer usable process settings from the overview.

## 3. Connect learning to a useful output

Read NIST's CHIPS overview [S2] and explore its links to packaging, metrology, and workforce resources. Choose one area of interest and write:
- The question you want to answer.
- The primary source you will read.
- A software-only or paper-based activity.
- The artifact you will produce.
- How another learner can check it.

Example: label die, package, and board in an original schematic and explain their different roles. This is an educational drawing, not an engineering design.

## 4. Complete and review

Use the worksheet. Completion criteria:
- All five stages appear in your map.
- Die, package, and PCB have distinct definitions.
- Testing appears at more than one stage.
- Every sourced claim has a traceable reference.
- Unanswered questions and simplifications are visible.
- Your next activity has an observable output and a check.
